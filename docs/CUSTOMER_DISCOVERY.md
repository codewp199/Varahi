# Customer Discovery Guide

Use this guide for short, respectful conversations with local business owners. The goal is to understand their workflow and priorities, not to pitch a predetermined website or app.

## Before the conversation

- Choose one business type to investigate at a time.
- Review the business's current website, listings, and customer-facing channels.
- Write down observations separately from assumptions.
- Ask permission to take notes; do not collect sensitive customer or business data.
- Keep the conversation to about 15–20 minutes.

## Opening

“We’re a small local team researching how independent businesses manage their online presence and customer inquiries. We’re not here to sell you anything today. Would you be open to a few questions about how this works for your business?”

## Core questions

1. How do new customers usually find your business?
2. What questions do customers ask most often before they visit or buy?
3. How do customers currently see your services, menu, products, hours, or availability?
4. What part of handling calls, messages, bookings, or orders takes the most time?
5. Can you walk me through the last time that problem happened?
6. What do you do today to work around it?
7. What have you already tried? What worked or failed?
8. What does this problem cost in time, missed opportunities, or frustration?
9. How important is solving it in the next three months? Why?
10. Who would decide whether to pay for a solution?
11. Do you currently pay for a website, software, marketing, or online ordering? What do you use?
12. If you considered a new solution, what would you need to know before buying?

## Ask for evidence, not compliments

Useful follow-ups:

- “When did that last happen?”
- “How often does it happen?”
- “What do you do next?”
- “How much time does that take?”
- “Have you paid to solve it before?”
- “What would make you decide not to buy?”

Avoid leading questions such as “Would an app help?” or treating “that sounds great” as evidence of demand.

## Close

“Is there anything I missed? Would it be okay if we came back with a short proposal if we find a practical way to address this?”

Ask only for a follow-up that the owner has agreed to. Do not imply a prototype or integration exists when it does not.

## Synthesis

After every 5–10 conversations, compare patterns by segment. Count repeated problems, urgency signals, existing spend, and concrete follow-ups. Preserve contradictory evidence. Do not convert a small sample into a market-size claim.
